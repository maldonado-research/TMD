# Native cell-state and mat-persistence evidence: a bounded source assessment

A newly inspected [version-one SBW25 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.27.702051v1.full.pdf), by Rodriguez-Sanchez, Rogers and Rainey, reports genotype-dependent transcriptomes and reporter-cell states, and a founding-density context for mat persistence. This strengthens the practical reason to measure genotype, culture context, establishment and recovery separately. It does not validate Mutation by Natural Dominance or identify a unique mutation mechanism.

The DOI was already an R26 metadata-only candidate. R36 adds primary inspection depth, rather than a newly discovered DOI. The preprint's header says this version was posted 27 January 2026 and was not certified by peer review; it states CC BY 4.0. A provider returned a 41-page PDF text representation. Claim-specific methods, captions and supplementary-table text were inspected. Original PDF bytes, figure-image values, raw RNA counts and author analysis code were not acquired or executed. ## What the measurements observe

The 14 WS mutants are attributed to the historical Lind et al. (2015) collection. Their shared WS phenotype does not define a new independent mutation-origin cohort. A new assay of historical genotypes can add observations, while leaving the origin histories and prospective holdout status unchanged.

Authors describe RNA expression from four independent biological culture replicates per genotype at 24 hours in static King's B medium, mapped to reference OV986001.1. These are source-reported culture/expression units, not authenticated native mutation births or rates. A mapping reference is not an independently sequenced actual stock. Figure 1's normalized mean GFP/mean mScarlet assay reports six independent replicates; its response is normalized fluorescence, without an absolute intracellular-copy calibration in this audit.

The preprint reports genotype-dependent Fap activity in a subset of measured cells. The flow-cytometry gates remove debris/nonfluorescent cells, so fractions concern the measured gated reporter population. Supplementary reporter descriptions state that some reporter insertions abolish native locus function. Reporter and deletion perturbations therefore require their own transport assessment before serving as native observers. These observations do not supply an independently calibrated event-inclusion or invisible-loss law.

The survival assay measures the fraction of culture mats remaining intact. It uses diluted 24-hour founder cultures, wells/microcosms and repeated time observations through 48 hours. The authors state that WS and derived fap-deletion pairs run alongside, while not all genotypes run simultaneously. Mat persistence differs from single-cell survival, single-new-mutant establishment and mutation-origin inclusion. Serial dilutions, repeated observations and shared-source cultures must retain their hierarchy.

The useful design implication is specific: genotype, founding density, early extracellular structure and culture/batch context can affect the observed mat outcome. Those factors belong in independently measured conventional alternatives and stage-specific controls. This source does not quantify a route-level transport bound, prove within-route homogeneity or justify replacing a complete frozen comparator with one fitness or fluorescence scalar. No change to the frozen model, theta/eta, error allocation or stopping follows.

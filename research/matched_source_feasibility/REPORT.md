## A common ancestor, with a specific sampling outcome

[Bantinaki et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.106.069906), Methods, reports WSA–WSZ from 26 separate microcosms founded from ancestral SBW25 SM. After five days, populations were plated on KB and one WS isolate was selected at random per microcosm, purified and stored. [McDonald et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.109.107110), Methods and Table 1, reuses and further characterizes that collection. These are **the same 26 isolate labels**, not 52 independent cultures. LSWS, AWS and MWS are additional mechanistic reference genotypes and are excluded from the WSA–Z collection count.

The source supports a common-ancestor, separate-microcosm sampling design at the reported protocol level. It does not provide the full exported founder, inoculum, batch, plate and eligibility ledger needed to certify the current study's count law. One random WS isolate at day five is an outcome conditional on phenotype and recovery. It is not the first mutation, first established lineage, dominant population genotype or a census of all origins. The collection is historical and already observed; publication or reinspection does not make it a prospective holdout. No unreported failed, excluded or no-WS run is assigned a zero count.

Our [derived molecular classifier](DERIVED_SOURCE_AUDIT.json) maps Table 1's 26 labels by the reported mutated gene:

| Molecular table category | Isolate count |
| --- | ---: |
| Wsp: wspF or wspE | 16 |
| Aws: awsX, awsR or awsO | 6 |
| Mws: mwsR | 3 |
| Unresolved: WSX | 1 |

McDonald reports that WSX depends functionally on Wsp, while repeated sequence inspection found no mutation in the three focal loci. A functional-dependency classification could therefore report 17 Wsp-associated isolates; that is a different classifier from 16 molecularly assigned Wsp isolates plus one unresolved. We preserve both facts and do not convert WSX into molecular truth. The source discussion also says four mwsR mutants among the 26, whereas Table 1 and the Results identify WSQ, WSR and WSZ, three collection members. MWS is a separate reference strain. Other text/caption label inconsistencies occur, including WSX versus WSR in the Figure 5 comparison. These are recorded as unresolved source inconsistencies, not repaired by importing an older aggregate or choosing whichever count helps a fit.

There is also a useful, limited allele cross-check. Table 1 gives WST as awsX Δ229–261, with an 11-residue deleted peptide. The Results explicitly identifies WST's in-frame deletion as **33 nucleotides**, spanning 229–261 and flanked by a six-nucleotide repeat. Thus 33 is a reported source value as well as the inclusive-coordinate length. Karita's R27 supplement labels a candidate awsX allele Δ32bp (229–261). The older source resolves WST's reported length; the cross-source annotation discrepancy and stock crosswalk remain unresolved. It does not authenticate the two stocks as identical, correct Karita's allele or select an actual study allele.

## What the exact Lind artifacts add

The fresh [official eLife figures page](https://elifesciences.org/articles/38822/figures) authenticates the `v1` download URLs for [Figure 6 source data](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.38822.015) and [Figure 9 source data](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.38822.021). R2's failures remain historical: those were denied shell calls and unverified candidate filenames. The changed prerequisites are this successful Firecrawl publisher path and the current enforced network policy allowing the CDN. Two bounded, TLS-verified public requests acquired **112,786 exact XLSX bytes**. No authentication, proxy change or unchanged denial retry was used.

Figure 6 contains three sheets with **46 Wsp, 41 Aws and 18 Mws mutation rows**, 105 total. The nine named columns describe mutation position, type, gene and proposed effect; they contain no culture, isolate, date or batch identifiers. Repeated identical mutation rows remain reported occurrences, not deduplicated mutations or newly proven independent origins. The four rare-pathway mutants reported by the article are absent from this focal-pathway workbook; their individual background allocations remain unresolved. This is not evidence that the source observed zero rare mutations.

The [prior article audit](../panel_eligibility/lind_sun/NOTES.md) remains decisive for context: each focal route was measured in a different double-deletion background with a cellulose-expression reporter. Authenticated workbook rows do not turn 46/41/18 into a common-background race, independent target-context supply or fixed-route recovery controls. Sun and Lind's later reuse of the Aws spectrum adds no independent cultures.

Figure 9 has **18 genotype rows and 73 visible coefficient cells**: seven control coefficients, four for each of nine other Wsp-labelled genotypes and five Aws-labelled genotypes, and three, three and four for the three Mws genotypes. The two three-value Mws rows lack a fourth value. The main Methods previously reported two excluded microcosms, but no exported IDs authenticate an exclusion-to-cell match. Missing values remain missing. The worksheet's A1 says “Figure 6 -source data” despite the official Figure 9 artifact URL; both identities are preserved.

For 17 of the 18 rows, the stored mean and standard deviation reproduce from the visible coefficient cells using the unweighted arithmetic mean and sample standard deviation. **Row 21, MwsR I844L, does not:** G21:I21 give a mean of −0.1082494198 and sample SD 0.0343654802, while D21/E21 store −0.0834227130 and 0.0151517203. These cells have no formulas. No hidden row/column or absent fourth replicate is invented to reconcile them. The source's intended calculation or correction remains unverified; this arithmetic mismatch does not establish a biological error. We retain its stored values separately, perform no inferential refit, and do not replace its summary. Pairwise competition coefficients in any case do not supply independent binary establishment or recovery outcomes.

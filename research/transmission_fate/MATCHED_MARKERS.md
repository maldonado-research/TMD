# Does matching each parental measurement to its own marker improve the score?

This secondary check was specified after the primary design and before reviewing the parental forecast scores. The roster consists of the same 17 mouse variants. For each marker the observed target r is its held-out non-WGS carrier fraction. A parent forecast p is its measured somatic fraction. The fixed reference q is the original ten-pup carrier fraction. The mean of q's loss minus p's loss gives the paired Brier gain using the same family and marker weights as the primary comparison.

First redistribute the parental fractions among markers within each family, keeping their measured values, WGS observations, and the non-WGS outcomes fixed. Calculate the expected gain under all such pairings and the exact fraction that reaches or exceeds the observed gain. Then restrict the possible pairings to markers with the same family and same observed WGS carrier count. The restricted comparison asks how much matching adds among markers indistinguishable by the ten-pup frequency. As a sensitivity also preserve parental identity within those strata. Report the number of strata with at least two markers and the number whose parental forecasts can actually vary. Repeat the restricted comparison using workbook carrier totals.

Summing over strata with total marker weights w, the mean paired gain exceeds its average across allowed permutations by 2 times the weighted sum of within-stratum population covariances of p and r. Against substituting the mean parental fraction within each stratum, the observed benefit of individualizing p equals the same covariance term minus the weighted within-stratum variance of p. This identifies how much of any observed advantage comes from putting the right measured parental frequency on the right marker. Enumerate exact within-family assignments using integer or rational arithmetic. A tail fraction here describes an exchangeability benchmark. Markers may trace the same developmental clone and somatic measurement reliability can differ across markers. The two pedigrees are the biological replication units.

# Predicting a recorded transmission in a different offspring panel

## Scope

The 0.14.0 study scored the class of an early substitution. This study asks whether a frequency measured in a parent or a discovery panel can predict measured carriage among other offspring in the same pedigree. The available development times, tissues and panels determine the endpoint. Parent tissue collection and offspring genotyping need not occur in chronological order. The archived studies measured transmission or presence. Selective advantage, competitive persistence and population establishment require different outcomes.

This retrospective specification uses known source margins and the previously published pedigree timing analysis. Those include the 388 validated events in two mouse families, the ten whole-genome-sequenced (WGS) offspring in each family, and aggregate early and late overlaps. Source selection and marginal counts preceded this specification. The comparative prediction scores and randomization tails are to be computed after this commit.

## Primary mouse comparison

Use the 17 source-designated early-embryonic single-nucleotide variants in the two large mouse pedigrees in Lindsay et al. (Supplementary Table 2). Match events to Supplementary Data 1. Use the reported parental somatic alternate-allele fraction as the parent forecast. Compare it with the fraction of the ten WGS discovery pups carrying the marker. The primary outcome is recorded carrier status in the other assayed offspring. Subtract the WGS carriers and the ten assayed WGS individuals only after confirming the roster and the native denominators. Give each eligible event equal weight within a family and each of the two families equal weight overall. Use Table 2 for assayed and carrier totals. Repeat with the supplementary workbook totals wherever sources conflict. Document membership evidence, missing results, ascertainment and any unverified per-marker callability assumption.

Score the binary Brier loss (p−y)² using recorded count k and tested n, giving p² + (k/n)(1−2p). Positive gain means lower mean loss than the stated reference. Primary contrast is parent fraction versus observed WGS fraction. Secondary contrasts include the smoothed WGS fraction (x+1/2)/(m+1), and a fixed 50:50 average of parent and observed WGS frequencies versus observed WGS alone. Report family-specific means, calibration (mean p versus observed k/n), and numbers of markers favoring each forecast. Use family counts as the replication units. These are paired descriptive comparisons in a discovery-selected panel.

## Separate checks

- For the other four mouse pedigrees use the separately parent-ascertained Supplementary Table 3. Restrict the scored endpoint to confirmed eligible candidates with a known tested count and carrier count. Distinguish confirmation, missing observation and an observed zero. Test parent fraction against a constant obtained from all other qualifying families' source-confirmed events (mean event transmission fraction per training family, then mean over training families), with equal test-family weight. Report any excluded and failed candidates and any outcome-conditioned confirmation. Where outcomes are available across the attempted roster, show the descriptive impact of including them without treating unconfirmed mutations as established truth.
- For the two large mouse pedigrees, compare whole recorded WGS profiles of five first-litter and five later-litter pups within each family. Fix the full ten-pup discovery roster and count the distinct validated events represented in both groups. Under the null, every balanced allocation of the ten pups to the two groups is equally likely within each family. Enumerate both families jointly, keeping all markers in each pup together. Report inclusive lower and upper tails and the two-sided tail defined by absolute distance from the null mean. Repeat conditional on early-group sex composition. Indicate what depth, litter-level biology, selection of loci and lack of random treatment assignment allow one to infer.
- In an independent plant source, prioritize confirmed parental natural mutations and genotyped offspring, including negative calls and missing genotypes. Inventory all amplified loci and their assayed denominators before any forecasting. Use tissue or branch allele fractions for comparisons only if measured for the full eligible roster independently of offspring positivity. If only marginal totals are public, bound the possible transmission frequency under missingness and identify which comparison remains unscorable. Keep the biological material and endpoint explicit, particularly the distinction between a seed embryo and parental fruit tissue.

The current sources and these design choices do not identify the first molecular mutant in the reproductive lineage or the fitness of its descendants. Any later diagnostic is to be marked exploratory.

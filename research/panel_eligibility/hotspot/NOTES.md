# Hotspot and repair evidence for matched-panel eligibility

Original source audit, 2 October 2026 (UTC). This component of TMDR000002 reads the two identified primary articles and the public TMD literature, provenance, prospective-plan and formulation notes. It is a targeted eligibility assessment for the current restricted matched Wsp/Aws/Mws supply–selected-outcome–recovery estimand. The studies remain useful for mutation-supply comparisons and biological alternatives. No matched W/A/M ledger is admitted from the observations inspected here.

## Horton, Cherry, Waugh and Taylor (2025)

*G_nT Motifs Can Increase T:A→G:C Mutation Rates Over 1000-fold in Bacteria*, *Molecular Biology and Evolution* 42(8), msaf183. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaf183), [primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12344412/). Primary NCBI XML verifies the four authors, DOI, PMID 40758847, electronic publication 4 August 2025, and CC BY 4.0 article license. Full article text and figure captions were inspected. The declared [OSF HSYFX](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/HSYFX) sequencing data/custom R script were not acquired: the API returned a proxy 403. The supplementary PDF request returned a browser challenge rather than a PDF. File-specific OSF licenses, replicate rows and supplementary annotations remain unchecked.

The experimental ancestor is immotile *P. fluorescens* SBW25 AR2 (ΔfleQ and inactive ViscB), with synonymous alterations around ntrB coding positions 289 or 683. These leave the protein sequence unchanged. Most constructs relocate ntrBC to a mini-Tn7 site in AR2 ΔntrBC; one construct uses scarless allelic exchange at the native locus. The paper explicitly discusses sensitivity to genomic position and replication-strand orientation. Thus the matched experimental comparisons establish strong sequence-context effects in this engineered motility assay, but do not establish the WS supply vector in a common WS ancestor or its transport to Pf-5.

The main experimental potency measure is the proportion of selected motile replicates carrying the hotspot mutation versus other viable motility mutations (Results §2.1–2.2; Methods §4.5, §4.7). Fisher odds ratios compare those selected proportions. Figure 1 reports 622 sequenced evolved lines across ten variants, grouped by G-tract length: 29, 112, 434 and 47. Those are selected genotyped lines, not known introduced singleton-mutant trials or a denominator of cell divisions. Per-motif hotspot counts are drawn in Figure 2; no exact per-motif numerator transcription was performed here. Figure 1 and Figure 2 overlap and cannot count as separate replication.

Methods §4.5 samples the first visible motile zone per plate within 24 hours, then sequences one passaged colony's ntrB locus. Three variants use four inoculated colonies per plate; that does not authorize counting four independent winners from each plate. Plates are discarded after sampling. Early motility may reflect mutation during pre-inoculation colony growth. The paper describes independently evolved populations, but underlying plate/founding-colony/batch IDs and unrecovered replicate rows were not checked. Absence of an ntrB mutation is not a fully identified alternative molecular route from ntrB-only sequencing.

Figure 5 separately reports seeded population totals 49, 52, 52 and 51 for single-hotspot, single-hotspot, double-hotspot and strand-reversed double-hotspot variants. Evolved populations sent for sequencing number 38, 43, 44 and 19. The remaining 11, 9, 8 and 32 did not evolve within eight days. These are population-level censored motility observations, not introduced fixed-route mutants that failed a recovery assay. Daily monitoring, post-mutation growth, potential pre-existing mutations, and censoring prevent relabeling emergence days as exact mutation or successful-arrival times. No prospective held-out WS context or frozen TMD parameter prediction is reported.

The quantitative >1000-fold comparison comes from reanalysis of inferred changes in natural *Salmonella* genomes (Results §2.3; Methods §4.6), rather than counting experimental cell divisions. Inferred T→G counts are divided by the weighted number of motif sites per genome, with Poisson intervals. Approximately 1.5 million inferred single-nucleotide changes, including >100,000 T→G changes, are not independent recovery trials. The G4T motif with 5′ GA and 3′ C has a reported relative rate near 1112 (95% CI 1004–1185), while 5′ AC and 3′ A is near 5.6 (2.668–10.23). These are relative, site-normalized comparative estimates conditional on the inference/sampling model; an absolute mutation probability per division in a specified WS strain is not supplied. The article additionally notes genomic-position effects and incomplete experimental verification of the second downstream nucleotide.

For WS baseline construction, Discussion §3.1 explicitly connects awsR A79C to the leading-strand motif CAG3TC and wspF T812G to GTG5TG. This justifies annotating exact ancestral sequence, complementary strand, G-tract length, both flanking dinucleotides and replication orientation before pooling mutations into route supply. It does not justify a universal Aws or Wsp multiplier, or borrowing the 1112-fold value for those different motifs.

## Torres and Alonso (2026)

The repository supplies the exact source, independently verified here: Rubén Torres and Juan C. Alonso, *Genetic dissection of DNA damage tolerance in Bacillus subtilis: RecA and recombination functions regulate translesion synthesis*, *Nucleic Acids Research* 54(13), gkag673. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkag673), [primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13335488/), PMID 42406627. Crossref labels 3 July 2026 as print/issued date and 6 July as online date; PMC XML verifies the latter electronic date. Both date labels are retained. The article is CC BY 4.0. Full primary text/figure captions and Crossref metadata were inspected; no guessed citation was used.

Methods §2.1 identifies BG214, a 168-derived *B. subtilis* strain lacking SPβ and ICEBs1, and isogenic repair/polymerase deletion derivatives listed in unacquired Supplementary Table S1. This supports repair-by-treatment comparisons in a shared strain family; it is not a single unchanged ancestor across all repair genotypes, an LB/SMMAsn replication, or a W/A/M competition panel. The source itself compares BG214 with different 168 derivatives and warns of prophage-associated mutagenesis differences.

Methods §2.5 reports 30 independent cultures per strain grown in LB at 37°C to OD560 0.8, untreated or exposed to 3 mM MMS for 15 minutes. Rif resistance is recovered on LB with 8 μg/mL rifampicin after 16–18 hours. The reported statistic is a mean RifR-colony/viable-cell frequency, even though the assay is called Luria–Delbrück. No per-division mutation-rate likelihood or generation/division denominator is described in the inspected calculation. Figures 2–5 captions instead describe at least five independent experiments and normalization to total CFUs from untreated cells, with a 10-mL exposed aliquot; Methods describe harvesting 50 mL and total viable cells. The culture–experiment hierarchy, aliquot mapping and exposed/untreated denominator must be reconciled from the numerical files or authors before use. A mean endpoint frequency cannot automatically be treated as a mutation rate.

For the spectrum, Methods §2.5 says at least ten independent RifR colonies per strain and condition were sequenced; Figure 6 and Supplementary Table S6 report resistance-allele composition. That is a source claim of clone independence, but the accessible text does not authenticate one isolate per founding culture, culture IDs, exact culture-linked sampling denominators, missing isolates or shared jackpots. Do not convert these colonies into independent culture winners. The data statement puts conclusion-supporting data in text/supplements and makes raw data available on request. The repository review reports a 14.8-MB ZIP; it exceeds this subtask's 10-MiB cap and was not requested. No S1–S6 numerical file was inspected or counted.

Figure 6/Results §3.24 report H482Y enrichment after MMS and repair-dependent spectra. The authors suggest fitness explanations for predominant H482 alleles, yet explicitly state that fitness costs were not assessed. Their mention that two alleles did not appear to impair LB growth supplies neither a competitive-fitness estimate nor a route-specific establishment probability. Chronic-MMS survival uses CFU ratios (Methods §2.3), independently repeated at least four times. Those population survival measurements, repair-mutant controls and protein-interaction controls do not constitute known introduced fixed-route singleton mutants with binary recovery outcomes. No frozen held-out route prediction is reported.

## What the quantities identify

Selected genotype proportions identify the distribution recovered by the particular assay. Under a justified constant successful-arrival model they can identify relative effective hazards; they do not uniquely identify mutation μ, accessibility A, establishment F or detection/recovery r. A selected hotspot/Other odds ratio can support a mutation-supply interpretation when competing supply and post-mutation selection/recovery remain comparable. Synonymous interventions, replication orientation, two motif neighborhoods and cross-species concordance provide substantive evidence for context-dependent mutagenesis. They still do not separately measure every post-mutation factor or authenticate the current W/A/M control-transfer model.

A causal route-specific mutation probability per division needs authenticated divisions/population history and mutation observations measured independently of selected route outcomes, or a validated fluctuation likelihood with explicit mutant growth, survival, phenotypic lag and plating assumptions. For treatment effects, record viable counts before/after treatment and distinguish mutation generation from selective survival. For the current matched panel, add fixed-route introductions with exact independent trial totals and binary recoveries on both supply and selected branches, plus a justified transfer model. Independently measured growth or chronic survival can inform alternatives; neither is automatically an establishment or recovery probability.

The actionable result is a set of requirements for stronger local-sequence and repair-dependent supply calibration and a precise request for missing culture/protocol metadata. No new supply baseline was constructed in this audit. None of the inspected rows passes the full current matched W/A/M eligibility gate; that conclusion applies to this estimand and inspected material, not to all possible uses or uninspected supplements.

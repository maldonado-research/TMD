# Lind/Sun matched-panel eligibility audit

Audit date: **2026-10-02 UTC**. Original source curation for queued TMD round R000002; no biological experiment, TMD hypothesis test, or new mechanism claim is made.

**Neither inspected study supplies an authenticated matched common-background, multi-context panel combining independent route supply, selected route outcomes and fixed-route binary recovery controls.** Lind's preselection mutation spectra and rate estimates remain useful in their stated designs. Sun and Lind reanalyse earlier experimental evidence and perform numerical experiments; their reused observations do not add independent biological replication.

## What the Lind methods establish

The main article's `s2-1`, key-resource table and `s4-1` identify SBW25 derivatives PBR721 Δaws Δmws (Wsp retained), PBR713 Δwsp Δmws (Aws retained), and PBR712 Δwsp Δaws (Mws retained), each carrying the pMSC cellulose-expression/kanamycin reporter. In `s4-2`, cultures grow in shaken KB for 16–19 hours at 28°C before kanamycin plating. This removes selection for occupation of the static air–liquid interface; it still includes a reporter and colony-recovery observation process.

Figure 8's caption states: “Only within operon comparisons are valid for this figure as the mutants isolated without selection had double deletions of the other operons.” Thus **46/41/18 are sample sizes from different pathway-isolated backgrounds**, not one multinomial of competing-route winners. Methods `s4-2` says: “One randomly chosen colony per independent culture with WS colony morphology was restreaked once on KB agar.” That supports the authors' culture-level sampling protocol. It does not supply exported culture, batch, well and isolate joins, which remain unverified without the workbook.

Results `s2-4` authentically reports **109 collected mutants = 105 focal-pathway mutants (46 Wsp, 41 Aws, 18 Mws) + 4 rare-pathway mutants**. The rare mutations are attributed in the text to PFLU0085/PFLU0183. This resolves the earlier absence of an article-level source for the aggregate four. The inspected aggregate does not identify their individual pathway/background allocation. All 109 cannot be relabelled as a common-background race; the four cannot be split between named genes by assumption.

Figure 2 and `s2-1` separately report mutation-rate estimates Wsp 3.7, Aws 6.5 and Mws 0.74 ×10⁻⁹, with figure-caption replicate counts 200, 200 and 400. The all-pathways-intact reporter strain has a total rate of 11.2×10⁻⁹ and caption n=100. These rate-assay replicate counts are not the 46/41/18 sequenced-mutant denominators, binary recovery-control totals or measured route probabilities from one background. Methods reports 60 independent wells per assay and repeated assays; multiplying 60 by the stated repeats does not directly reconcile the Figure 2 totals. Workbook records and any exclusion mapping are needed. This audit neither invents that inventory nor treats the unresolved mapping as inferential invalidation of the source study. The article also cautions that its between-strain t-test assumptions about total population size were not met (`s4-2`).

The selected mutation-spectrum comparison in `s2-7` refers to McDonald et al. 2009; its Wsp 15/24 fraction is retained only as a historical pointer. This audit does not reconstruct a full selected triad, resolve a different older aggregate, or authenticate a new matched selected collection from that fraction.

Fitness methods `s4-4` describe 1:1 competitions against a GFP-marked WspF ΔT226-G275 reference, 24-hour static KB growth, before/after ratios, selection coefficients and independently inoculated quadruplicates. GFP marker and deletion/reporter-cost competitions are real controls for that fitness assay. They are **not** the required baseline/selected × W/A/M fixed-route controls with known introduced units and one binary recovery outcome per unit. Methods reports exclusion of two microcosms with >5% smooth colonies; exported per-strain replicate completeness is unverified. The paper itself cautions about frequency-dependent fitness (`s2-8`). Competitive performance does not directly identify establishment probability.

## What the Sun/Lind methods establish

Methods `s6` explicitly states: “We only use experimental data published previously, described in Appendix B.” It reports Python numerical completion, estimation and repeatability experiments. Empirical-data section `s2-2`, Appendix B `s8-3`, Table 3 and reference `R21` link the 41 Aws occurrences to Lind 2019. The attributed factual Table 3 transcription contains 12 mutation categories totaling 41. It is reuse of the same Aws collection, not 41 new cultures or new selected outcomes.

Appendix B `s8-2` and Table 2 estimate 500 possible mutations across 16 genes using incomplete empirical and molecular-function evidence. The authors say: “It is not a statement about the true number of WS mutations per gene”. These are proposed target counts, not observed isolates. Numerical assumptions of independent random mutation draws cannot authenticate culture independence. The assumed average rate and calibrated mutation-rate distribution in `s3-10` are not context-matched independently measured W/A/M baseline trial counts. No new selected collection, fixed-route binary recovery panel or whole-context biological holdout is supplied by the inspected article.

## Exact missing-measurement map

`MISSING_MEASUREMENTS.json` and `missing_measurements.csv` provide twelve study-by-component rows with required fields, evidence locators and explicit scope. Missing values stay null; absence is never encoded as zero.

| Component | Concrete measurement still needed |
| --- | --- |
| Common background | Strain, ancestor, genotype, reporter and route availability proving that W/A/M compete in one documented background in each declared context. |
| Context-matched supply | Independent, validated route-probability calibration for each selected context; exact baseline denominators, units, culture IDs, uncertainty, hotspots, repair and temporal metadata. A deletion-specific fluctuation rate is not a matched categorical baseline count. |
| Selected route outcomes | Locked selected endpoint and route rules, culture-level W/A/M/Other outcomes, exact eligible denominators, exclusions and missingness. Existing spectra and pairwise competitions cannot fill these counts. |
| Independent cultures | Founding-culture, isolate, lineage, ancestor, mutation-step, block and plate/well joins; identify prior collection reuse and dependent measurements. The documented protocol is useful; exported joins are unverified. |
| Fixed-route recovery controls | For each context, baseline/selected × W/A/M: known introduced L>0, recovered integer 0≤k≤L, explicit unit and one binary outcome per introduced unit, justified binomial marginals and relative recovery transfer—or independently justified prespecified mismatch bounds. |
| Whole-context holdout | The public TMD plan specifies ≥4 distinct conditions and ≥1 reserved whole condition, preferably a later block. Freeze models and stopping rules; predict both shared θ and the new context's η before selected outcomes. Fitting η to held-out winners supplies only the weaker contrast check. |

This is an **article-level absence/eligibility assessment within the inspected scope**, not proof that every unpublished or unacquired file lacks relevant measurements. In particular, source-workbook fields remain unknown. Neither paper supplies authenticated exact first-successful-arrival/censoring records in this audit; endpoint isolates, 16–19-hour culture durations and 24-hour competition endpoints cannot substitute for those times.

# How often did the two libraries identify the same final leader?

The original and repeated libraries identified the same final leader in 53 of the 70 wells with both complete measurement series. They identified different leaders in 17. These are two measurements of material collected from each well at the same visit. A change in the measured leader between these libraries gives no timing information about a biological reversal.

Fasanello and colleagues selected samples with high cross-contamination in the original library and reprocessed aliquots from the same collections. Across the 66 reprocessed library samples in the paper, the authors reported mean cross-contamination of 0.75% in the original measurements and 0.05% after reprocessing. They chose the library with lower measured cross-contamination for each repeated sample. That choice operated on pooled samples, so the wells within a pool share the library choice. The authors' 66 samples span multiple experimental assays and sampling times. The 70 wells here are a distinct unit. Selection for high contamination limits any extrapolation from this comparison to other wells or studies. The study has no independently evolved replicate population for either of these two readings of the same well. ## Inputs and reproduction

`technical_counts.tsv` preserves all 76 wells from the source longitudinal table at days 0, 10 and 25, corresponding to generations 0, 100 and 250 in the study's nominal schedule. A and B follow the same anonymous orientation as the main counts table. Blank pairs indicate an unavailable library visit. Integer zeros are observed values. `selected` identifies the library used by the authors at that well and visit. The 228 selected pairs exactly match the corresponding main-table counts. The source assigned 77 wells to the two-label design and removed one containing its reference identifier. The supplement also includes separate assays with different label counts per well. [Author supplement at the pinned commit](https://github.com/VinceFasanello/MM_Code_Supplement/tree/a370be7497fda328c6c8b13d957223ff4cba53c6/2_CountstoAnalysis)

Run `python technical.py` with standard Python 3 to regenerate `technical_results.json` on standard output. `technical.py` checks the SHA-256 of `technical_counts.tsv` before scoring. Among the 76 wells, the selected library yields 45 baseline, 55 early-leader and 57 early-change strict hits. The original library alone yields 57, 60 and 58. Only the matched 70 comparison holds population membership fixed across both original and repeated libraries.

To rebuild the normalized counts, use Python with `rdata` (tested with 1.1.0) and pandas. In a checkout of the source repository at commit `a370be7497fda328c6c8b13d957223ff4cba53c6`, supply the checkout path to `python read_technical.py PATH_TO_CHECKOUT technical_counts.tsv`. The extractor verifies SHA-256 for the original and rerun metadata, four native expected/unexpected count matrices and the published `myevo.Rdata` table. It replays the authors' lower-contamination choice for each visit, with the original library retained on a tie. It then verifies all 456 selected raw label counts against the published table after reversing its single added pseudocount per label. The raw assigned counts determine the forecast. The pseudocount enters the authors' library quality comparison. The checked TSV SHA-256 is `619c7d99a261304a5117fa24530573a2dabc2f7deca0aea812a1c2ce00a522bb`.

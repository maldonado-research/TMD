# R33: Implemented DNA calibration resources and their entry stages

Two published methods provide concrete resources for assessing absolute DNA abundance: Bian et al.'s [barcoded spike-in-based calibration (BSINC)](https://doi.org/10.1128/aem.02179-25) and Tourlousse and Sekiguchi's [synthetic rDNA-mimics](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismeco/ycaf028). Their primary methods and public deposit metadata change the calibration resource position beyond a future spike-in proposal. Neither establishes native SBW25 mutation supply, establishment, or recovery, and no TMD input is admitted.

## BSINC: an implemented library-stage calibration

The [published primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12997851/) describes a log-distributed DNA standard containing eight bacterial and two fungal strains. Standard and sample DNA are split across three barcodes, pooled and sequenced together. These library barcodes identify sample partitions; they do not authenticate independent biological replicates or heritable mutation ancestry.

The authors account for barcode effects using observed sequencing output relative to output expected from input DNA mass, then fit theoretical against observed standard genome-copy numbers. The main text reports observed copies based on normalized mapped bases divided by covered bases. Exact equations, standard composition, quantities and uncertainty details refer to the unacquired supplement. The commercial standard and fitted relationship provide a concrete calibration architecture; they do not establish independently certified stock counts or a native transfer here.

Digital PCR supplies a useful author-reported comparison on selected genus-level 16S targets, using **aliquots of the same purified DNA** as sequencing. This corroborates downstream quantitation under the tested conditions. It does not independently test cells lost before or during extraction, and gene-to-genome copy conversion has not been replayed. The authors explicitly retain extraction bias, rare-taxon sequencing requirements and reference-database completeness as limitations. Their broad mock-community validation used species from distinct genera, leaving closely related strain discrimination unresolved.

The source's 10% coverage-fraction detection criterion and 10% coefficient-of-variation quantitation criterion are assay criteria, not universal cell detection probabilities or bounds on invisible mutation births. Its dynamic sequencing decisions do not replace TMD's frozen stopping rule.

The project query reports 218 public SRA experiment records: 215 Illumina and three Nanopore. Only the first 20 general summaries were retrieved; a platform-specific query retrieved all three Nanopore summaries: [SRX30880760](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX30880760), secondary effluent; [SRX30880596](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX30880596), mixed liquor; and [SRX30880595](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX30880595), mock community. The mock summary displays 95.4 million bases while the paper describes a 4.78-Gbp validation experiment. Their correspondence needs a barcode/sample/replicate crosswalk; different processing or aggregation may explain it. No discrepancy is treated as proof of error or absent data. Actual sequence files and the article-linked [workflow repository](https://github.com/Harper19/wf-metagenomics_test) were not acquired or run.

## rDNA-mimics: an earlier DNA entry stage with a small named deposit

The [primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11912825/) reports 12 synthetic rRNA-operon constructs, two including bacterial SSU-V4 sequences. Constructs were linearized, purified and quantified by a fluorescent dsDNA assay; Sanger sequencing verified sequence identity. These are DNA concentration and identity checks, not direct independent counts of introduced living cells. Mock-community benchmarks also used cloned rRNA genes in plasmids. Copy-input uncertainty and preparation accuracy remain relevant to any prospective transfer.

For soil, the detailed methods and results specify that standards were added **after cell lysis by bead-beating**, to avoid shearing. The abstract's broader phrase “prior to DNA extraction” must therefore not be read as a pre-lysis whole-cell recovery experiment. This earlier entry stage can probe downstream DNA recovery and amplicon processing, subject to how well naked standards match sample DNA. It cannot measure differential cell lysis or loss before its entry. The source reports primer-dependent biases and omits extremely low or overwhelming spike-in fractions from particular analyses; no numerical threshold or performance guarantee is transported here.

An acquired [correction](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismeco/ycaf112) changes the original publication date to 11 February 2025, rather than 17 March. It states no scientific measurement correction. The primary paper names sequence accessions PQ159721–PQ159732 and BioProject PRJNA1100950; those sequence records were not acquired. The separately acquired Zenodo metadata confirms the public version DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14592209 and lists `data.tar.gz` (280,012 bytes), `rmarkdowns.tar.gz` (8,757,587 bytes), and `graphs.tar.gz` (995,973 bytes), with CC BY 4.0 metadata. The advertised MD5 values have not been checked against downloaded archives. The small data archive is a specific next artifact for inspecting actual counts and input crosswalks; its contents are not yet claimed.

## Prospective use and preserved boundaries

The useful distinction is the stage at which known material first shares the assay. BSINC co-sequences separately barcoded extracted DNA; rDNA-mimics can share post-lysis extraction and amplification. A standard cannot by itself quantify events lost before its entry, and matched processing of standards and native material still requires evidence. Genome copies, rRNA gene copies, physical cells, viable cells and mutation births remain distinct quantities.

A separately fitted calibration remains potentially admissible given identifiable parameters, joint uncertainty, a justified count law, native-context transfer and a freeze before evaluation. Calibration from an evaluation endpoint cannot also serve as that endpoint's independently frozen predictor. Nothing here diagnoses the earlier HiDenSeq fit as intrinsically invalid or identifies a unique mutation mechanism.

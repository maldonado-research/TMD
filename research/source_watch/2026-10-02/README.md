## 1. A supply intervention in the same SBW25 organism

Farr, Vasileiou, Lind and Rainey, *An extreme mutational hotspot in nlpD depends on transcriptional induction of rpoS*. Published 31 January 2025; [10.1371/journal.pgen.1011572](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011572).

The primary article reports 153 evolved SBW25 Δwss populations screened for cell chaining: 139 yielded that phenotype, and 137 contained the same C565T substitution. Reconstructed alternative mutations did not all show a detectable fitness disadvantage. The study then separated a selectable promoter reporter from the native fitness-altering gene. Reported fluctuation point estimates were 4.2×10⁻⁷ and 7.2×10⁻⁹ mutations per base pair per replication in SBW25 and ΔpsrA reporter backgrounds, respectively: approximately a 58-fold difference. The transcription/PsrA contrasts support a supply mechanism in that engineered system. The precise chemical mechanism remains unresolved.

This is a stronger competing explanation than assigning repeated adaptation to an unidentified dominance force. It also creates a specific transport question: the reporter sits away from native nlpD, and the authors explicitly retain uncertainty about the native-site rate. Growth-phase dependence, genotype effects and assay selection need measurement. Reporter cultures used colony-size thresholds and nested sequencing confirmation; their estimates depend on fluctuation-model assumptions. An embedded supporting caption reports a small reporter fitness reduction, so neutrality should not be treated as exact.

These are cell-chaining observations in a Δwss background chosen to suppress WS phenotypes. They cannot become W/A/M competing-route counts. Public [dataset metadata](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14335473) identifies a 7,880,751-byte figure-data ZIP and CC BY 4.0 license. Only that metadata was acquired; neither ZIP contents nor its reported checksum were verified against a download. The archive is a useful next acquisition, not an admitted panel.

## 2. Population size does not remove supply effects by itself

Barber and Couce, *Mutation-biased adaptation is consequential even in large bacterial populations*. Published 10 June 2026; [10.1038/s41467-026-74044-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74044-6).

The study combines simulations with serial cefotaxime evolution in two *E. coli* repair-mutator backgrounds across five culture-size regimes. The observed endpoint patterns depend on mutation-spectrum alignment with beneficial substitutions. Its simulations show that pathway lengths, baseline rates and fitness assumptions can change how the influence of mutation bias scales with population size. These simulations illustrate an established population-genetic alternative; their outputs are not measured biological route probabilities.

The 75 Sanger isolates were chosen among the longest-surviving lineages, and one isolate can contain changes in both target genes. This is a survival-conditioned endpoint collection, not a mutually exclusive first-arrival race. The simulation comparison also adjusts effective population size downward; it is not a frozen forecast of unseen contexts. For TMD, record extinction/no-qualified outcomes, population history, route aggregation and multi-step genotypes, and avoid assuming that a large culture makes measured supply unnecessary. Raw source-data files, sequence reads and the linked simulation code were not acquired or executed. The article is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; these files contain original critical commentary, without its figures or full text.

## 3. Mutation production differs from phenotypic visibility

Li and Gyorgy, *Tuning evolvability via plasmid copy number and regulatory architecture*. Published online 31 December 2025, although the PMC collection year is 2026; [10.1038/s41467-025-67995-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67995-9).

Engineered *E. coli* plasmid experiments and stochastic models connect copy number, regulatory architecture and detected fluorescence or resistance. Complementation can mask coding-region loss-of-function changes, while promoter changes can remain visible. Regulatory buffering and detection thresholds can even make the observed gain-of-function fraction nonmonotonic in copy number. Those findings concern a genotype-to-phenotype mapping; they do not mean that every change in recovered mutants is a change in DNA mutation production.

The flow protocol splits a diluted overnight culture among eight wells and records at least 100,000 events per sample after regrowth. Neither eight wells nor that many events automatically establish independent founding-culture or known-introduced recovery trials. The engineered EvolvR/plasmid system differs from native chromosomal W/A/M evolution; no parameter is transferred. Plasmid masking also concerns established genetic dominance. TMD's operational “natural dominance”—a route's propensity to yield a specified successful heritable outcome in a context—does not identify that dominance relation or an additional causal force.

## Unchanged sources and the next discriminating question

The two previously inspected mutation-bias preprint Crossref records are byte-identical to their prior snapshots. Their empty version relations provide no changed evidence and do not prove that a journal version cannot exist elsewhere. A same-title anaerobic mutation preprint and journal record in the search results are conservatively grouped into one evidence family. None of these copies is counted as independent replication.

The useful next question is: **Can a native-background, genotype-validated, time-resolved supply assay distinguish mutation production from reporter transport and delayed phenotypic visibility before selected W/A/M outcomes are examined?**

A measurement contract should preserve exact ancestral sequence, promoter/repair state, growth phase, culture and block ancestry, sampling times, genotype validation and predeclared phenotype gates. Measure introduction, establishment and recovery separately. Pair native-site checks with reporter controls and short- versus long-recovery windows; changes in those windows need to be interpreted through growth and survival as well as detection. Keep Other/no-qualified outcomes explicit. Compare the frozen TMD forecast with route-specific supply and measured establishment/recovery alternatives using the same information. New unknowns remain unknown; these sources do not fill the prospective study's 26 unresolved fields.

## What the paper changes for the TMD assessment

The authors develop approximate fixation probabilities for a conventional mutation-and-selection process in a regime between rare-origin dynamics and deterministic large-population behavior. Their strong-selection treatment retains continuing mutation input while a previously established lineage grows, and lets the competitor's establishment chance change with the current population composition. Their weak-selection treatment uses a different approximation because an established fitter lineage need not then be guaranteed to prevail. Agreement with their Wright–Fisher simulations concerns this declared model; it supplies neither biological calibration nor TMD-specific evidence.

The useful scientific distinction is between first establishment and eventual fixation. A separately measured single-introduction establishment probability can be meaningful, yet the eventual winner can also depend on subsequent mutation origins and competition during the sweep. Consequently, failure of a simple supply-times-establishment endpoint comparator would not, by itself, favor a distinct natural-dominance mechanism. It could reflect an omitted ordinary interference process. This adds a concrete model-adequacy question to the existing TMD warning about clonal interference; it does not change the existing estimator or forecast.

The new source does not establish the TMD common-curvature restriction, a common theta, a target-context eta predictor, or a biological mutation count law. It asks for ultimate fixation among explicitly enumerated genotypes. TMD's current primary target is a held-out score gain per observed qualifying W/A/M endpoint, with its actual endpoint and measurement frame still unresolved. These are different estimands until an admitted observation map connects them.

## Why this is not a replacement W/A/M comparator

The model starts from a monomorphic ancestor without standing alternatives, uses a constant-size haploid asexual Wright–Fisher population, permits two alternative single-step beneficial mutations, and forbids subsequent mutations from those mutant states. The strong-selection approximation additionally requires meaningful separation between competitor fitnesses. Actual serial transfer, shifting antibiotic exposure, multistep genotypes, recombination, unobserved losses, phenotypic detection and technical recovery require separate justification.

The paper's extension to more than two alternatives is expressly limited: Appendix C gives the least-fit genotype's fixation probability under the strong-selection approximation. The Outlook explains why a full probability vector for all competing genotypes is harder and is not supplied. A single marginal probability cannot be promoted to a complete strictly positive three-route forecast. In particular, using two pairwise formulas and normalizing them would require a new justification; this review provides none.

The illustrative comparison with Schenk and colleagues uses parameters inferred from the historical endpoint study and is described by the authors as oversimplified. It is not a preregistered held-out TMD benchmark. No native parameter is transferred from it.

## Concrete next decision

Before selecting a future empirical protocol, ask whether independent lineage observations support a rare-origin regime in which later competing establishments are negligible, or instead demonstrate coexistence and interference before the declared endpoint. Obtain population/division and bottleneck histories together with allele-resolved lineage trajectories, recorded independently of held-out winner fitting. These observations should distinguish new origins, descendants, missing lineages and detection failures. A large nominal population or a route-frequency curve does not answer this regime question.

If interference is material, a complete conventional comparator must predict the same observed outcome vector from independently admitted mutation, demographic, fitness and observation inputs, then be frozen before held-out endpoints. The present preprint offers a bounded theoretical reference for that development, not the missing three-route benchmark. No full null comparator, empirical rate, new count law, threshold, error allocation, stopping rule or actual registry choice is selected here.

This is an actionable refinement of the expert review question, not a new experiment completed in R24.

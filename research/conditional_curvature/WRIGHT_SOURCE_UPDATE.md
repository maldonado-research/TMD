# Wright 2026: primary-text measurement clarification

Original source assessment with AI assistance · 5 November 2026

The official full article upgrades the previous abstract-only assessment of Wright et al., *Resistance mutation supply modulates the benefit of CRISPR immunity against virulent phages in Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, [DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.3004094](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3004094). This is a source-definition audit, not a remeasurement or numerical replay.

The modified fluctuation assay describes 12 colony-founded replicate populations subdivided across eight phage treatments. Its reported “probability of resistance” is the fraction of challenged replicates yielding colonies; “mutation frequency” is the ratio of bacterial CFU density with versus without phage. These observations combine antecedent variation, challenge survival and colony recovery. They do not independently count mutation births, measure a mutation rate per division, or separate establishment from recovery. Shared founder ancestry across treatment subdivisions must remain distinct from independent founding units.

The separate efficacy experiment uses 24 colony-founded replicate populations per strain, with each replicate plate containing 351 phage treatments. Treatment wells within a replicate therefore share ancestry. Efficacy compares growth with and without phage; the escape breakpoint was selected by visual inspection of the observed efficacy distribution. The different assay frames and outcome definitions cannot be pooled into one independently calibrated supply–establishment experiment.

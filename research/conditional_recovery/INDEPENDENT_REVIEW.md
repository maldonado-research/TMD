# Independent mathematical and computational review

5 November 2026. AI-assisted internal review, not external peer review or a biological result. The existing repository was read only; computations and this review were prepared in a separate scratch directory.

The proposed two-context conditional recovery sensitivity calculation is mathematically coherent under its declared observation and interval-coverage contracts. It does not reproduce the published methods 0.6.0 interval-intersection calculation: its object is a fixed reference-statistic tail over a tilted finite conditional law. The older residual-bias identity and its scientific limitations remain applicable. No claim of mathematical priority is supported or needed.

## Law and factor of two

For positive route-specific baseline-to-selected recovery ratios rho, every log rho vector decomposes into a common normalization term, a W-minus-M tilt, and an A-curvature term. The latter is delta_c=log rho_cA−(log rho_cW+log rho_cM)/2. Conditioning on pooled route totals, each selected N and W-minus-M difference, and total selected A removes the unknown baseline probabilities, common biological theta and the two nuisance tilts. The remaining factor is exp((delta_2−delta_1)A_2). Because A_2 advances in steps of two, setting j=(A_2−min A_2)/2 gives t=exp(2(delta_2−delta_1)) and conditional mass proportional to h_j t^j. Equivalently t=m_2/m_1 for m_c=rho_cA²/(rho_cW rho_cM). A missing factor of two would define the wrong null family.

Common recovery curvature cancels even if full recovery maps differ. Arbitrarily estimated recovery controls do not establish a bound on this parameter. In particular a residual ratio relative to fixed-route controls is not the raw rho unless the full control contribution has been included. The observed count model requires a joint product multinomial law; paired or descendant dependence cannot be justified by the marginal distributions alone.

## Fixed-statistic validity and exact optimization

For any fixed t and fiber, h_J is a scalar statistic and p_t(J)=P_t(h_K<=h_J) is its inclusive lower-tail probability. Its attainable lower-tail events are nested, so P_t(p_t<=a)<=a for every a. This argument allows ties and uses no likelihood ordering at the tilted law. Taking the supremum over a fixed interval that contains the true t preserves the conditional bound because the supremum is pointwise no smaller than the true-parameter p-value.

The proposed exact endpoint maximum has a valid special structural proof. Feasible local A values form a contiguous parity interval; intersecting the two local supports after fixing their sum leaves contiguous j. Each h_j is a product of six positive binomial sequences sampled at affine integer arguments, so it is log-concave. Hence C={j:h_j>h_observed} is a contiguous interval [a,b], or is empty.

Writing Q(t)=P_t(C), the numerator of Q'(t) is the sum over i in C and j outside C of (i−j)h_i h_j t^(i+j−1). Terms with positive coefficients have degree at most a+b−2; negative coefficients have degree at least a+b. If both tails are present, division by t^(a+b−1) gives a strictly decreasing function, so Q can turn only from increasing to decreasing. With one tail it is monotone, and with empty C the requested tail is identically one. Thus 1−Q is quasiconvex and its supremum on every finite positive interval is exactly the greater endpoint value. The implementation checks support, log-concavity and complement contiguity before applying this result.

This does not license arbitrary endpoint maximization: h=(2,1,2), observed at the middle index, has p(t)=t/(2+t+2t²), endpoints 1/6 on [1/2,2], and interior maximum 1/5 at t=1. The proposed helper refuses its disconnected strict complement. Reflection of h together with t in [1/u,1/l] leaves the result unchanged. Multiplication of all h by a positive common factor also cancels. Rational interval endpoints bound the full continuous nuisance family, including irrational true t; they do not discretize it. Numerical transformations used to construct such endpoints must preserve coverage by outward enclosure.

## Statistical bounds and the extra allocation

For a random interval C with valid marginal noncoverage at most a fixed gamma, p_adjusted=min(1,sup_C p_t+gamma) is a valid marginal p-value. When alpha>=gamma, rejection on the coverage event implies p_true<=alpha−gamma; adding the probability of noncoverage bounds rejection by alpha. When alpha<gamma rejection is impossible. Independence between the interval and the observed counts is unnecessary for this union-bound argument. Marginal coverage alone does not give conditional validity on every fiber. A deterministic covering interval, or suitable coverage conditional on the fiber, supplies that stronger statement.

The comparison receipt includes an exact stress test with primitive h=(24,1), true t=1, and a deliberately outcome-dependent interval: point 1 for J=0 and point 1/100 for J=1. Marginal noncoverage is exactly gamma=1/25. The unadjusted p-values are (1,1/2401), giving false rejection 1/25 at alpha=1/100. The adjusted values are (1,2426/60025), and all their attainable thresholds satisfy superuniformity. This is a mathematical counterexample to omitting interval uncertainty; it does not propose outcome-dependent calibration for a scientific study.

## Executed checks

The independent raw-law oracle imports no producer, prior inference, or geometry implementation. It enumerates every four-arm count table for baseline and selected totals four in each of two contexts: 50,625 tables and 49,329 fibers per law. It uses nonuniform baselines q_1=(1,2,3)/6 and q_2=(3,1,2)/6; observed tilt factors 3/2 and 2/3; common curvature factor 2; and differential-curvature factors 1/2, 1 and 2, giving true t=1/4, 1 and 4.

These are realizable shared-biological-curvature nulls. Set every baseline recovery probability to one, selected recoveries in context 1 to (1,1,1), and selected recoveries in context 2 to s(1,rho,1), where rho is the stated differential-curvature factor and s=min(1,1/rho). All recoveries then lie in (0,1], context 2 has delta_2=log rho, and both biological selected laws have curvature log 2 relative to their biological baselines.

For each of those three laws, raw joint multinomial masses sum exactly to one, every reconstructed conditional mass agrees with h_j t^j after normalization, and every attainable robust-p threshold is valid for three containing intervals. There are 151,875 full table/law evaluations and 454,122 attainable-threshold checks across the three laws (151,374 thresholds per law). A further 77,550 rational-grid comparisons check endpoint domination for all 285 distinct raw weight sequences encountered, together with interval-reflection symmetry. These finite checks substantiate the implementation and examples; the mathematical arguments establish the general claims within the bounded model.

The separate producer comparison checks all 50,625 observed tables against independently constructed weights, observed indices, reference probabilities, robust probabilities, gamma-adjusted probabilities and inclusive decisions. It also checks malformed values and explicit refusal of the disconnected-complement counterexample. The receipt identifies the precise producer hash tested and refuses a source change during the run. The final polynomial arithmetic guard is conservative: after clearing an endpoint denominator, each polynomial has at most degree times endpoint-bit-length plus coefficient bits plus a term-count allowance. The 12,000-bit cap leaves room for the subsequent 256-bit gamma adjustment below Python's standard decimal-conversion limit.

## Synthetic findings and scope

For the existing symmetric witness h=(1,576,1), either extreme observation gives p_reference=1/289. The robust p is 17/2321 over [1/4,4], and 257/9473 over [1/16,16]; both reject at alpha=1/20 with gamma=0. Over [1/32,32] it is 1025/19457, which does not reject. These are hypothetical sensitivity scenarios, not measured recovery bounds or power calculations. A supplied interval excluding one can decrease the reference p; only nested sets guarantee monotonicity. For wholly unrestricted t>0, the supremum is one because a minimum-weight endpoint is in the reference event and exponential tilting can concentrate there.

The fixed reference test is not claimed to be optimal and differs from recomputing a probability ordering separately for each tilted law. Nonrejection is compatibility under the stated model, not equivalence, biological mechanism identification, an all-category forecast result, or evidence that recovery controls transfer. Scientifically justified positive observation laws, external recovery bounds, a study-level error allocation and fixed analysis/stopping choices remain necessary for confirmatory use. The wrapper does not modify frozen forecasts or repair those missing study inputs.

The implementation is acceptable within those limits. This review found no remaining mathematical or numerical blocker after the endpoint structure proof and the interval noncoverage adjustment were included.

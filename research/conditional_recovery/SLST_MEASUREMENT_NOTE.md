# SLST-Seq: assignment and composition controls do not identify native recovery

Orcel et al.'s [UMI-guided single locus sequence typing method for phylotyping Cutibacterium acnes from skin samples](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2026.1807759), published 20 May 2026, is a newly inspected source in this continuation. Selected Results, Discussion and Methods were read directly from the acquired official full [PMC13231282](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13231282/) JATS; its 126,193 bytes match SHA-256 `8e86d14d30a55fa672ec950379d03a7d3b57df2ece2ea297f2b5720fb09c7cb4`. No author code or underlying sequencing data was run or reanalyzed. `SLST_SOURCE_REVIEW.json` preserves the public identity, source hash, section anchors and inspection limits. The [discovery receipt](DISCOVERY_RECEIPT.json) records the bounded PubMed query, seven returned records and inspection depths; this was not a systematic review.

The paper reports that, across six single-isolate DNA controls, an average of 93% of sequences were assigned to the expected SLST type; the remainder includes both unassigned sequences and a small fraction assigned to other types (Results, `s2_1`). This is a conditional assignment summary among observed sequences, not a known-introduced-cell recovery probability, per-route sensitivity interval or complete confusion matrix. The off-type assignments make the distinction between loss and wrong labels concrete. In a defined DNA mixture, G1 was observed at 26.4% against a nominal 33.3% (Results, `s2_2`); the Discussion identifies consistent G1 underrepresentation as suggesting amplification bias. The ratio of those percentages is a compositional distortion summary, not absolute G1 recovery: all types share the normalization denominator, and the output also includes assignment and eligibility steps.

The calibration boundary is experimentally specific. Methods `s4_4` describe clinical skin-strip DNA being pre-amplified before molecular barcoding, whereas mixes and controls enter the barcoding step as purified DNA. The Figure 4 caption in `s2_4` explicitly states that SpikeMix samples lacked pre-amplification. Thus the source's general statement that mixtures share the library preparation, sequencing and bioinformatic pipeline does not establish equal processing across the entire clinical workflow. A standard added at a later stage cannot alone calibrate earlier sampling, extraction or amplification loss. The source also pools counts from independently processed *technical* library/sequencing replicates to estimate mixture proportions (Methods, `s4_2`); those replicates and their sequences are not independent introduced-cell or mutation-birth denominators.

The useful contribution is a measurement example: pure known-type controls reveal assignment errors, known mixtures reveal relative distortion, and recording the entry stage exposes a transport question that an aggregate accuracy percentage misses. These controls concern C. acnes/C. modestum DNA and SLST labeling, not matched baseline/selected Wsp/Aws/Mws outcomes in the proposed contexts. They supply no empirical bound on `t=exp[2(delta_2-delta_1)]`. No source percentage is transferred into the TMD sensitivity calculation, and no empirical rejection, biological effect or medical recommendation follows.

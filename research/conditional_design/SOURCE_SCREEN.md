# Native-organism source screen

Original assessment with AI assistance · 5 November 2026

Two official PubMed searches covering publication dates from 1 January 2025 through 5 November 2026 returned 15 records for `SBW25 OR "wrinkly spreader"` and three for `"Pseudomonas fluorescens" AND ("mutation bias" OR "mutation supply" OR "establishment")`. All 18 distinct titles/metadata were screened; detailed abstract assessment here is limited to the following two leads and the previously assessed Karita source. This is a bounded search, not a systematic review or evidence that other useful studies do not exist. The [receipt](SOURCE_SCREEN_RECEIPT.json) preserves literal queries, returned identifiers, URLs and acquired-representation hashes. Raw article text is not redistributed.

**Contamination and organism identity.** *Metagenome sequences of unintended bacterial communities from a Pseudomonas fluorescens experimental evolution study* ([PMID 42937301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42937301/), [DOI 10.1128/mra.00853-26](https://doi.org/10.1128/mra.00853-26), electronic publication 27 October 2026) reports 16 metagenome sequences from contaminant communities in an experiment initiated with SBW25. The abstract describes center/edge locations from eight populations in uniform or heterogeneous glucose agar environments and four primary genera. These counts describe metagenomes and source populations, not independent mutation origins or Wsp/Aws/Mws trials. This motivates authenticating organism/strain identity before interpreting environmental route differences. It does not establish contamination in any TMD candidate dataset; data files and full methods were not acquired here.

**Background-dependent compensatory routes.** *Compensatory evolution facilitates loss of prfB autoregulation in Pseudomonas fluorescens SBW25* ([PMID 42186845](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42186845/), [DOI 10.1093/molbev/msag125](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msag125)) reports two classes of compensation after engineered frameshifting defects: changes in ribosome-associated proteins and single-nucleotide deletions in prfB. The abstract says eliminating autoregulation alone had no detectable fitness effect. This supports matching ancestral genotype and quantifying ordinary compensation as competing explanations. It does not demonstrate directed mutation production, supply independent native-route offsets or validate TMD. Full text and numerical data were not inspected here.

The search also returned Karita et al., [DOI 10.1098/rspb.2025.2004](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2025.2004), already assessed in the repository's bacterial-panel and establishment-control audits. It remains the same evidence family. No complete matched native baseline/establishment/recovery/outcome panel was identified or admitted from this bounded abstract screen and the existing Karita assessments. No frozen study input, forecast, stopping rule or method is changed.

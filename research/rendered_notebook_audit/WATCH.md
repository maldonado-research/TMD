# What BASIL can measure, and what remains unseen

Ricardo Maldonado · 16 October 2026 · Original assessment with AI assistance

[Kuo and Kryazhimskiy (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013946) present BASIL, a Bayesian filtering method for estimating lineage fitness from barcode trajectories. Their primary article offers a useful measurement lesson: better inference of fitness among detected lineages still leaves mutation births, invisible losses and detection incomplete. This assessment authenticates the article representation and selected claims. It does not reproduce the authors' analysis or adopt BASIL as a TMD comparator.

The [claim and paragraph record](BASIL_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT.json) binds 12 claims to 28 exact paragraph hashes from PMC12974954.1. The article was published on 27 February 2026 under CC BY 4.0. The XML also contains four peer-review sub-articles; those are excluded from these scientific anchors.

The paper's simulation benchmark starts with 3,000 adapted lineages among 100,000 barcoded lineages. Their mutations are already present, and no new mutations arise during the simulations. This gives known selection labels for the simulated comparison. It does not test recovery of newly arising mutation births. The authors also show that neutral-reference selection and noisy read counts can bias fitness estimates, especially under strong selection. Their comparison with FitMut2 concerns that implementation and these test conditions; it does not establish a corresponding error in the original FitMut artifact inspected in R40.

The measurement calibration deserves its own inclusion record. The authors started with 26 yeast clones and prepared ten sequencing libraries. They excluded one low-coverage replicate, two unidentified barcodes and two undetected low-frequency barcodes, leaving 22 barcodes observed in nine replicates. They report overdispersion that increases with read abundance and allow the noise parameter to vary across intervals because experimental details may matter. The retained calibration supports an observation model for those measurements. It does not establish the recovery probability of absent barcodes, all lost biological events, or a transferable native calibration constant.

BASIL's adaptive-lineage calls also depend on an empirically chosen confidence factor. The paper selects 3.3 using performance on its two simulated regimes, with a precision–recall tradeoff; it explicitly says effectiveness on real data is difficult to establish. Roughly 15–20% of adapted lineages in those simulations remain uncalled. A neutral classification therefore cannot be used as independent evidence that no adaptive event occurred, and the reported benchmark precision cannot replace a frozen native false-positive calibration.

The real-data checks answer narrower questions. Fitness measurements from 410 sampled yeast clones provide a comparison target, while the authors acknowledge that those clones can differ from the corresponding original adapted lineages. Reducing coverage or sampling frequency tests sensitivity against full-data analyses, whose calls are themselves inferred. A mean-fitness consistency check likewise uses inferred fitness and frequencies. These checks do not independently identify mutation ancestry, establishment times, missing events or the complete distribution of mutation effects.

The authors describe further boundaries: a constant selection coefficient per barcode can miss within-barcode polymorphism; later secondary mutations may undermine inference; and fluctuating or frequency-dependent selection and spatial structure require additional work. They explicitly state that BASIL does not infer the beneficial mutation rate, which would require accounting for mutations lost while rare and established mutations that were not detected.

For TMD, the useful next step is an independently justified measurement and evaluation frame: named lineages and sampling stages, preserved exclusions and undetected categories, separate reference and recovery evidence, and known limits on invisible losses.

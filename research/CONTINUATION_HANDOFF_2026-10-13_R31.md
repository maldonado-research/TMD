## A recent measurement lead and its calibration limits

Husain, Flagg, Pincus and Murugan's HiDenSeq preprint, DOI 10.64898/2026.08.03.742544, version one posted 7 August 2026, uses DNA barcodes and sequencing to sample fluctuation-distribution shape. The inspected version is not certified by peer review. The main PDF provider reports all 11 pages; supporting information, raw data and code were not acquired or reanalysed. Canonical page/frontmatter posted dates agree; an auxiliary January citation-publication date is retained as a metadata disagreement, not used as the posting date.

The demonstration is yeast canavanine resistance, not an SBW25 WS experiment. Its 119,672 tracked barcodes are author-reported lineage labels in pooled growth, with approximately ten founder cells per barcode and computationally constructed virtual lineages. Those numbers do not become independent TMD culture or mutation-event counts. Reads are converted to absolute mutant numbers using a total count estimated by colony counting and refined during fitting. That fitted scale is not silently admitted as independent native supply. Reported postselection non-detection cannot alone exclude births followed by loss or poor recovery.

The authors explicitly warn that mutant fitness differences and competition can distort barcode frequencies. Their stable canavanine frequencies cannot establish WS frequency independence or native transport. The method is useful for a qualified feasibility question about independently calibrated route-specific lineage measurements. Barcode identity, founder ancestry, grouping/dependence, absolute calibration, event genotype/ancestry truth, exposure and named-stage establishment/recovery/loss measurements still require actual records. The main document promises code/data in a public repository without naming one there; this is not a global absence claim.

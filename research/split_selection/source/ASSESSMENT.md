# How the peach study selected sites

Ji et al. collected the two flowers in May 2019 and published the study on 18 August 2025. Twenty microsamples from Flower #2 were lysed, then the lysate from each was divided into two equal fractions for separate processing and sequencing. The split was technical. Flower tissue architecture, sampling and lysis preceded it. A one-sided observation can arise from how material enters or is measured in the two aliquots. [Primary article, Methods and Figure 2A](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829) · [Figure S2C, preparation diagram](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s002)

Figure S3D explicitly says all 108 analysis-ready alignment files were processed together for discovery. These included both aliquots of each split microsample. The preliminary list contained 1,888,276 sites. Comparison with controls and filtering on local quality left 179 candidates for visual review, of which 70 remained. Table S2 assigns 37 to Flower #2. The same 40 split libraries appear again in the technical comparison of those 37 sites. The other flower and earlier canopy leaves served as controls for floral specificity. They could exclude a candidate without discovering its presence independently in Flower #2. [Study Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12370204/) · [Figure S3D–E, discovery diagram](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s003) · [Table S2, candidate origins](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s016)

## Two descriptions of the read threshold

The Methods say the screen excluded sites with “no sample with at least 5 reads” and name the option `--min-supp-depth 5`. The authors’ summary for this study in Table S13 says “>5 reads with mutation in at least one sample.” This identifies alternate support as the intended quantity and leaves a discrepancy about equality at five. Figure S3E also lists a total-depth exclusion across samples, distinct from alternate support. Controls, strand bias, neighboring insertions/deletions, repetitive regions and final visual review contribute to selection. The archived Table S3 contains the reference and alternate counts for retained sites. It does not supply intermediate caller support and manual decisions across rejected candidates. [Primary Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12370204/) · [Table S13, last study row](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s027) · [Discovery diagram](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s003)

Across the 70 Flower #2 libraries in Table S3, all 37 retained flower sites have at least five alternate reads in one or more libraries. Eight have a maximum of exactly five. A strict six-read minimum applied to these deposited counts would exclude those eight known retained sites. The inclusive five-read condition is compatible with all 37. We use that condition as a proxy for one step. The original decision for every candidate cannot be reconstructed from these summaries. [Table S3, deposited counts](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s017)

At 26 sites, at least one of the 30 unsplit Flower #2 libraries already has five alternate reads. At another three sites, the observed pooled counts and split depths guarantee that one half meets the threshold whatever the allocation. The other eight sites each have exactly one comparison with two or more alternate reads and exactly one pair that can reach the threshold. For four of these sites, passing the proxy requires all alternate reads to fall in one half. These observations explain why an unconditional benchmark can understate expected one-sidedness. Holding other counts and the two depths fixed, we can condition the allocation at the one informative pair on the site passing the proxy. Summing the site-local benchmarks gives 7.13070634 expected one-sided comparisons, compared with 3.17859655 under unconditional allocation and 14 observed. Conditioning simultaneously on every site’s original inclusion would require additional assumptions about dependence across sites and the other discovery decisions.

## What the earlier canopy data contribute

The study’s Table S2 lists 48 loci from 75 branch leaves with the source year 2016 and citation Wang et al. (2019). The original Wang et al. catalogue contains exactly 48 DHQ1 entries. Matching chromosome and position recovers all 48 Ji attributions. The reference and alternate states agree for all 46 single-nucleotide changes. The two remaining 2019 entries are an insertion and a deletion. Ji marks two earlier sites (M71 and M101) as present in both flowers. The 2019 catalogue lists them in two and 14 leaf samples respectively. It attributes two other deep markers used in Table S7 (M119 and M120) to the current paper. Neither position occurs in the 2019 catalogue. Table S7 supplies actual read counts for the featured markers. Its marker set was selected for lineage comparisons, including an examination of their signal in the flowers. The 46 further previously published branch loci marked absent in both flowers lack matching detailed counts in that table. Aggregate absence labels cannot fill an unobserved depth or alternate count. The four branch-marker measurements give context for recovery in the same flower. Their inclusion and the 2025 data release predate TMD’s November 2026 prospective archive specification. [Original 2019 catalogue, S2 Data](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.s019) · [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191) · [Table S2](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s016) · [Table S7](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s021)

The original authors also used Sanger sequencing to assess the four featured branch markers in Flowers #3–5 and a neighboring leaf. These additional plant materials and markers do not yield truth labels for the original 37-site by 20-lysate grid. The published study and supplements were inspected on 19 November 2026 (Pacific). [Validation measurements, Table S10](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s024)

### Reproducing the historical match

The companion TSV transcribes the 48 rows where the 2019 S2 Data individual is DHQ1 from the publisher’s rendered spreadsheet. The fields are chromosome, position, the authors’ mutation type and the number of semicolon-delimited identifiers in the Samples column. The authors retain credit for those data. The 2025 comparison uses the original Table S2 workbook with a checked byte digest. In the directory containing the script, run `python3 -B verify_wang_markers.py /path/to/pgen.1011829.s016.xlsx` and compare the result to `wang_marker_receipt.json`. The 2019 rendered rows were inspected directly. The original 2019 workbook bytes were unavailable in this replay.

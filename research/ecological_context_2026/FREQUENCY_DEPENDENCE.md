# Frequency-dependent fitness source assessment

Ricardo Maldonado · 27 October 2026 · Assessment with AI assistance.

**Decision:** Ascensao et al. supply a useful, independently published challenge to transporting a single relative-fitness number between frequencies, competitors or growth windows. Their phase-resolved results sharpen the existing TMD ecological-control contract. They do not supply native Wsp/Aws/Mws mutation supply, lineage-establishment truth, recovery denominators, a complete comparator, or a test of frozen TMD.

Source: Joao A. Ascensao, Keon D. Abedi, Aditya N. Prasad and Oskar Hallatschek, *Frequency-dependent fitness effects are ubiquitous in rapidly adapting microbial populations*, **Science Advances 12**, eaeh3295, 23 October 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh3295); [PMID 42912986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42912986/); [primary full text, PMC13693659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13693659/). ## What was measured

The Results subsection **“Beneficial mutations exhibit pervasive frequency-dependent fitness effects”**, Fig. 1 and Methods **“Frequency-dependent fitness measurements”** describe five single and ten double mutants reconstructed on the LTEE ancestor REL606. The mutations are proxies for the first five beneficial mutations to fix in **one** LTEE population, Ara-1: glmUS-promoter mutation, rbs-operon deletion, pykF deletion, spoT SNV and topA SNV. This is a selected reconstructed panel, not an unbiased sample of new mutations or 35 independently evolved populations.

There are 35 pairwise comparisons: 15 mutants versus REL606 and 20 double-mutant versus immediate single-mutant comparisons. The reported counts are 2/5 single-mutant comparisons and 26/30 involving double mutants with measurable frequency dependence; together these give 28/35, or approximately 80%. Approximately 70% of comparisons show negative frequency dependence at P < 0.01 after FDR correction; three show significant positive dependence. These percentages describe this panel and assay. They are not a measured prevalence for SBW25, all mutations, or all microbial environments.

Each pair was mixed at nominal volumetric fractions 0.01, 0.2, 0.8 and 0.99. Flow cytometry measured actual frequencies at the end of one growth cycle and after the next daily 1:100 transfer into DM25. The response is the change in logit frequency over that cycle, **s = logit(f1) − logit(f0)**, with relative Malthusian fitness also analyzed. Typical replication is four to eight biological culture replicates per pair and frequency. These are culture-level competitions involving many cells, not single-new-lineage establishment trials. Flow-cytometer events, repeated times and shared mutant genotypes do not multiply independent evolutionary replication.

Methods **“Quantifying fitness effects”** describe a median/MAD outlier rule and median aggregation; **“Quantifying frequency-dependent slopes”** uses orthogonal distance regression with errors on frequency and fitness. Reusing numerical results would require the actual retained replicate/exclusion map. Methods **“ANCOVA model”** estimate an across-comparison association: invasion fitness explains about half the model-estimated nontechnical variation in frequency-dependence slopes. That variance decomposition is not a held-out native-route forecast. The authors also report refitting slopes without invasion-frequency points (Fig. S10); that supplement was not independently acquired here.

## The concrete new control insight

Results **“Frequency dependence is structured within the growth cycle”**, [Fig. 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13693659/#F5), and Methods **“Within-cycle time courses / Coculture experiments”** compare each single mutant against REL606 under minority/majority conditions. Nominal 10/90% mixtures were grown together for an additional day before baseline measurement; actual baseline frequency need not equal the original volumetric fraction. The overnight coculture was diluted and distributed into three replicate wells per condition. Thus the source calls these three biological replicates, but they share a preceding coculture; they are not three independently evolved founders. Samples were taken approximately hourly through 10 hours and again at 24 hours.

Three single mutants, G, S and T, had no significant net whole-cycle frequency dependence while showing opposing phase-specific frequency effects that canceled over the cycle. **A net null is therefore insufficient evidence of phase-invariant competitive effects in this source.** It is not evidence that hidden effects must exist in every other system. The derivative curves are derived from averaged trajectories, finite differences and smoothing, with bootstrap uncertainty; they are not direct molecular measurements of a mechanism. The primary text reports that late-exponential effects account for roughly 10–40% of absolute per-cycle frequency dependence among the examined strains.

Results **“Fitness relationships violate transitivity assumptions”**, [Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13693659/#F4), and Methods **“Quantifying nontransitivity”** show failures of predicting a direct pairwise fitness difference by subtracting two fitness measurements against a common reference. Their operational nontransitivity is a nonzero additive residual across differently composed pairwise competitions. It should not be relabeled as demonstrated rock–paper–scissors cycling or a measured three-way-community outcome.

Useful candidate controls, to be assessed within the existing R27 contract rather than commissioned here:

1. Independently measure a focal genotype's competition response across relevant starting frequencies and resident competitors, retaining actual frequencies, total density, introduction timing and growth-window identity. A 1:1 assay against one reference is not automatically a rare-invader calibration.
2. Where the proposed biological claim involves time or establishment, include phase-resolved observations alongside the declared endpoint. This can test whether an endpoint null conceals opposing effects. Existing TMD baseline/selected phases must not be confused with physiological lag/exponential/stationary growth phases.
3. Compare directly measured pairwise responses with predictions from a shared reference under matched conditions. Preserve the competitor and population composition in the transport key. Pairwise agreement still does not establish a complete multiple-origin or three-route outcome model.
4. Carry culture/founder/batch clustering, sampling-related changes, marker controls and observation uncertainty into admission. The article reports no measurable marker effect (Fig. S4) and quantitative agreement between glass-well and flask competitions (Fig. S5), but those supplementary controls were not independently inspected and cannot certify markers or geometry in another organism.

## Predictive and mechanistic limits

The source's simple consumer-resource model explains one contribution: frequency-dependent resource exhaustion changes the duration over which growth-rate differences accumulate. The primary Results explicitly state that this alone changes per-cycle **s** but does not produce frequency dependence in Malthusian fitness **w**, or change strain ranking. It accounts for a median of roughly 20% of resolvable slopes and cannot generate positive slopes. Other physiological explanations in the Discussion, including lag costs and later compensatory substitutions, are proposed interpretations rather than established causal decompositions. Fixation-time increases are simulation results, not observed fixation histories in these assays.

This paper can strengthen the case for independently parameterized, composition-aware conventional alternatives. It cannot supply such an alternative for TMD merely by substituting its fitness values, its approximately 80% prevalence, or its ANCOVA relationship. A conventional comparator must predict the same complete outcome vector using admitted native inputs and be frozen before held-out evaluation. Predictive improvement by frozen TMD would remain incremental observed predictive performance; it would not identify a new mutation mechanism. Failure of a constant-fitness shortcut is not failure of ordinary evolutionary explanations.

Native mutation birth, inherited/pre-existing status, exposure, losses before observation, operational establishment, and separate recovery/classification remain unmeasured by these pairwise assays. The source also differs from the proposed SBW25 setting in species, reconstructed mutations, medium, mixing, daily transfer and ecological context. Discussion explicitly distinguishes daily batch temporal niches from continuous culture; even its continuous-culture comparison is an expectation, not a tested result.

Existing TMD material already records frequency dependence and reference-competitor limits in `research/reporting_record_assessment/REPORT.md`, and resident state, density, timing, competition and independent establishment/recovery in `research/establishment_controls/REPORT.md`. The incremental value here is the experimentally illustrated **whole-cycle cancellation** and **direct-versus-reference-predicted competition check**, not discovery of those broader gates. No change to frozen theta/eta, study values, count law, comparators, error allocation, stopping or methods 0.6.0 follows.

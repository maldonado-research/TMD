# Site-specific reads in the parental leaves and calls in the progeny

Companion Table S7 covers all 75 original leaves and includes the missing focal measurements for M101 and M71. See [native S7 correction](S7_CORRECTION.md).
Ji et al.’s native S11 workbook contains seven first true leaves from progeny and 26 branch leaves from the parent peach tree. The original sheet spans A1:P37. All parental sample identifiers match the 2016 DHQ1 B1 roster. The three original B1 samples absent from S11 are DHQ1_B1-1-L1, DHQ1_B1-1-L2 and DHQ1_B1-L1. The parental rows report reference and alternate read counts and variant allele frequency. Their genotype call fields are empty. The progeny rows have explicit genotype calls. The measured read counts supply evidence for three memberships that were left unresolved in the earlier catalogue study. [Original workbook](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s025) · [Figshare file metadata](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/29935289) · [Wang carrier catalogue](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.s019)

For M101, alternate-read counts alone separate all 12 originally listed leaves from all 14 originally unlisted leaves among these 26 samples. Each listed leaf has three to seven alternate reads. Among the 14 unlisted leaves, two have zero, three have one and nine have two. The 2019 study documented a support rule for a mutation shared by multiple samples: at least one sample should have five supporting reads and other carrier samples should have at least three. Strand, quality, manual assessment and other filters also entered the original discovery process. The M101 counts are consistent with that threshold. A single cutoff on allele fraction could not achieve the same separation, since the listed B1-14-L2 has 3 of 77 alternate reads (3.90%), below the unlisted B1-4-L1 with 2 of 41 (4.88%). At M103, all 26 parental leaves were originally listed, and every row has 12–32 alternate reads. [Wang et al., Methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191) · [Ji et al., marker IDs and coordinates](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829.s016)

Three of the ten entries shared by the two 12-addition model completions have a measured count in S11. All involve M101. B1-11-L2 has 0 of 70 alternate reads, B1-6-L2 has 2 of 60 and B1-8-L2 has 2 of 51. Among those three predicted entries, two reach an exploratory threshold of two alternate reads. Seven of the eleven other unlisted entries also reach it. At a one-read cutoff the counts are two of three and ten of eleven, respectively. At three reads both groups have zero. Weak read support thus extends well beyond the named completion cells. S11 supplies no independent genotype call on these parental leaves. Neither the displayed zero nor the one- or two-read counts settle whether a rare population of cells carries the marker. The named focal leaf B1-1-L1 remains unmeasured in S11. [Earlier exact predictions](https://github.com/maldonado-research/TMD/blob/b96446a969e066f4835fba0ce49412100a388343/research/leaf_lineages/completion/orientation_result.json)

The seven progeny have explicit M101 calls of Absent in all seven. At M103 the calls are four HET, two HOM and one Absent. At M120 they are three HET, two HOM and two Absent. Four progeny have both Group 2 markers, two have only M103 and one has only M120. The authors collected seeds from B1 in 2019, grew them in 2020 and noted that the pollen source was uncontrolled. These are outcomes in seven selected descendants of one parent. The paper had already reported Group 2 marker transmission. Counting allele copies or reads cannot determine which lineage arose first. [Ji et al., Methods and Figure S12F](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829)

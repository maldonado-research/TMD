# Which mutation group was first at a sampled yeast generation?

Johnson et al. followed laboratory baker’s yeast populations for about 10,000 generations. The archive records allele counts and sample times in the same populations. We scoped this reanalysis to the 18 initially haploid populations selected for sequencing in the standard rich-medium, 30 °C condition (12 MATa and six MATα). Their six sequencing visits were at generations 70, 1,410, 2,640, 5,150, 7,530 and 10,150. First means the earliest sampled visit satisfying a specified rule among the archived nuclear small-variant groups. [Article, Methods and Supplement 2](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.63910) · [Sample metadata](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/63910/elife-63910-supp1-v2.xlsx) · [Original per-population records](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/63910/elife-63910-supp2-v2.zip)

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Local frequency at least 10% | 2 | 16 | 2 | 17 / 1 |
| Deposited `present_at` flag | 4 | 14 | 5 | 17 / 1 |
| Local frequency at least 90% | 6 | 12 | 7 | 15 / 3 |
| Deposited `fixed_by` flag | 8 | 10 | 9 | 14 / 4 |

The first 10% visit preceded or equaled the first 90% visit for every population, but the labels depended on whether later measurements entered the definition. In well P1E11 a missense group (145) was the only group with a local 90% observation at generation 70. It dipped below 90% at generation 5,150, reaching the deposited fixation criterion only at generation 7,530. A synonymous group (55) was the only first deposited fixation and qualified at generation 1,410. The corresponding pattern strings are 222122 and 022222, where 2 means at least 90%, 1 means 10% to below 90%, and 0 means below 10% at an adequately covered visit. This example concerns mutation-marker records in one population. A synonymous mutation could have traveled with another change on the same genetic lineage. The records do not measure the cause of growth, identify two competing clones or date the mutations’ origins. ## Presence defined using later data

The article describes a mutation as present at a visit if depth is at least five reads and the alternate frequency is at least 10%. Its archived processing script writes the `present_at` columns using the `fixed_by` function at that lower threshold. A covered qualifying visit must have no later adequately covered value below 10%. Previous high-frequency fixation also carries over. The two definitions can give different labels for the same visit. In the 34,554 row-by-visit comparisons there were **1,372 local 10% observations unflagged in the deposited presence field** and **447 deposited presence flags at visits with depth below five**. The raw-count reconstruction matches every source presence and fixation flag under the archived code's logic. The first sampled date for presence agrees across all 18 populations, while the set of earliest groups differs in 14, and the set of their annotation classes differs in nine. Ignoring the later fall below threshold would characterize a different endpoint. [Archived code, definition](https://github.com/mjohnson11/VLTE_PIPELINES/blob/588043a94abb34b13a6dd7a1b25277c25ae8deaf/WGS/process_files_and_run_go.py#L223) · [Archived assignment](https://github.com/mjohnson11/VLTE_PIPELINES/blob/588043a94abb34b13a6dd7a1b25277c25ae8deaf/WGS/process_files_and_run_go.py#L445)

The authors use 90% for their haploid fixation threshold, subject to depth of at least five and no later adequately covered sample below 90%. The first fixation visit then carries forward even through low depth. A last-visit crossing needs no later confirmation by this definition. These endpoint rules do not establish persistence beyond the last measurement. The first pass also includes the source's P1B03, an initially haploid population that autodiploidized. Excluding it leaves 2/17 wells with a unique group at the first local 10% visit and 7/17 with a unique group at the first deposited fixation. [Exclusion in the authors' code](https://github.com/mjohnson11/VLTE_PIPELINES/blob/588043a94abb34b13a6dd7a1b25277c25ae8deaf/WGS/process_files_and_run_go.py#L418)

## Observational unit and limitations

We restricted Supplement 2 to nuclear chromosomes I–XVI and the five deposited small-variant group classes (missense, nonsense, indel, synonymous and noncoding). We excluded telomeric records, required at least five alternate reads across visits and one or both of the two source ascertainment filters. The filters use counts collected over the full series, so even the locally scored crossings are conditional on retrospective discovery. We kept all 5,759 qualifying records, spanning 5,543 within-population mutation groups. Nearby changes can belong to the same group under the authors' definition. A group qualifies when any qualifying record in it meets the rule. Group IDs are local to each population. Published classes describe annotation, and unphased population sequencing can put several nominal competitors in one clone. The 18 populations shared a laboratory context and are not 18 measurements of a single underlying first mutation.

A 2026 study performed a separate time-series experiment in fission yeast and compared it with the earlier budding-yeast study. The budding-yeast observations in that comparison reuse the Johnson collection. Its definition of a high-frequency change, sampling times and organism differ. [2026 primary comparison](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-026-03017-1)

Venkataram et al. provide a comparison with actual lineage barcodes. They sampled 4,800 clones from the two original populations at generation 88, identified 4,009 distinct barcodes, and selected 418 clones for genome sequencing out of 3,883 unique barcoded clones with assigned fitness. Selection favored high-fitness clones. About 1% of cells were already diploid at the start, and 451 of 1,649 diploid lineages had previously been inferred adaptive across both replicates. The authors considered diploidy preceding the replicate split as one explanation. These observations confirm that lineage labelling and clone characterization can coexist with genuine uncertainty about which heritable change arose first after a defined start. [Venkataram et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.08.002)

## Reproduce

The reduced TSV gives the well, starting mating type, marker group, group annotation, the six local threshold states and the two archived flags. A question mark denotes depth below five. The offline calculation uses the Python standard library. The optional extraction from the two original supplements uses openpyxl and checks both SHA-256 digests. It reconstructs the flags from integer read counts without retaining genomic identities. Run these commands in this directory.

```sh
python3 -B yeast_events.py | diff - yeast_result.json
python3 -B yeast_events.py --extract elife-63910-supp1-v2.xlsx elife-63910-supp2-v2.zip | diff - yeast_observations.tsv
```

# Additional tests fixed after the main study design

The Fasanello main benchmark had already been scored when these comparison rules were written. The new comparisons were selected on source design. Their own scores had yet to be calculated. Both reuse public observations. Results that share wells, founder stocks or reference measurements must be identified.

**Separate founding assay.** Use the 91-label proof-of-concept diploid assay from Fasanello et al., a separate measurement library. Match founder labels to the 21 wells in the standard diploid condition after reversing the documented duplicate-label suffix where applicable. Use all ten matched technical repetitions in the unweighted log-rate contrast from the main forecast, with the common-reference counts cancelling within each repeat. Use the 20-generation measurement interval and a 250-generation target. Evaluate baseline, assay-only and combined leader hits, gain direction, and baseline and combined mean absolute share error on exactly these wells. Report how often the two assays agree on the faster founder and how often each forecast matches the endpoint. The same source populations supply the outcomes. [Source archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3888967)

**Separate populations in another yeast study.** Use Khristich et al.’s plain M3 medium only. Use the most recent author-deposited count table available for this check, document its checksum, data version, founder roster and any source exclusions. Use the twenty biological populations with common transfers 0, 2 and 63. Train on replicates R1–R10 and test on R11–R20, then reverse the roles. This is a split by source replicate number. Record whether it also separates acquisition batches. Within a given population at a visit, pool technical counts for the same founder before normalizing to shares. Use a documented study founder roster only. Score the largest measured endpoint share over that entire roster. Report any unassigned counts or excluded rows that affect interpretation.

Compute each founder’s mean share across the ten training populations at transfers 0 and 2, giving each training population equal weight. The change factor is the mean at transfer 2 divided by the mean at transfer 0. Require positive transfer-0 training share and positive holdout starting share to assign a rate forecast. A zero at transfer 2 gives a zero change factor. Report primary hit counts for (1) the focal population’s baseline leader, (2) the largest training change factor alone, (3) the largest focal starting share times the change factor raised to 63/2. Include the training transfer-2 leader as a comparator that observes exactly the same training visits. Withhold every postbaseline observation in a test population from its own forecast. Score against the measured winner at transfer 63, including any ineligible training label, and report ties as abstentions. Print aggregate scores only. The twenty outcomes belong to two shared forecast groups. This tests transfer of an early abundance change across populations of known founders. It does not replicate the separate common-reference assay in the main benchmark. [Khristich et al.](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.09.681448)

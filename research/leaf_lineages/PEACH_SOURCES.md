# What a listed carrier means in the peach catalogue

Wang et al. sampled 75 leaves on a single *Prunus persica* sapling called DHQ1. The seed germinated in 2015, when the authors identified four main branches. Additional subbranches developed in 2016. Figure 4 describes 43 sequenced leaves from the base-branch diagram and the remainder from small branches. The Results say the leaves were collected in September 2016, while Figure 4 gives 24 July 2016. The consistent resolution is the collection year. Table 1 calls the DNA source “leaf.” The study generally extracted DNA from a whole leaf or part of a leaf and also used a separate microsampling design on HY1, a different peach tree. A DHQ1 record identifies a leaf sample in which a variant was reported. Each leaf combines cells. [Primary study, Results, Methods and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191) · [Figure 4, original sampling diagram](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.g004) · [Catalogue S2 Data](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.s019)

The study used two discovery approaches. One contrasted groups identified by branching topology. The other compared subsets of samples through the frequencies of observed alleles. It excluded alleles present in more than 80% of the samples in that frequency analysis. The authors inspected candidate calls manually, described missed genotypes, and explained that early variants shared across all branches could escape detection. Figure S1 explicitly allows the recorded topology to be corrected if the pattern of calls reveals a recording error and the pipeline to be rerun. This makes a test of the source list against the source topology partly circular. S2 Data provides a list of reported carriers. An unlisted sample can be counted as outside that list, but ruling out mutant cells in the physical leaf requires additional evidence. [Primary Methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191) · [Calling diagram](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.s001) · [Examples of missed and ambiguous calls](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.s008)

The Results report 48 DHQ1 mutations and assign 11 shared mutations to the first year. The supplementary lists contain 14 sites reported in at least two leaves. Three of those sites each occur in two leaves of one named shoot. Grouping leaf labels by the prefix before their terminal leaf number leaves 11 sites spanning multiple shoot labels. They account for 108 listed occurrences, and the 34 singleton sites plus three within-shoot doubletons account for 40. These counts reproduce the authors’ reported partition by year under their biological interpretation. Membership across shoots can be checked in the catalogue. Year of mutation was inferred. They give 27/29, 14/29 and 8/29 leaves as examples from branch B1 and explicitly infer a hierarchy of cell divisions. A later paragraph describes a “profile of 29 mutations” without reconciling that number to the 48-site inventory. The authors also assign some mutations to the 2015 or 2016 growth periods using observed sharing and the branching history. Those year assignments depend on that model. Counting equal, nested, disjoint and crossing carrier lists examines an existing biological interpretation at the resolution of individual reported co-occurrences. The leaf labels alone cannot assign an absolute age to a mutation. [Primary Results and Figure 4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.g004)

A 2026 *Nature Plants* study used experimental single-cell lineage tracing in *Arabidopsis*. In the plants examined, the authors found descendants of three early founder lineages in the branches, flowers and siliques they studied. Different cell lineages can therefore be represented in one visible organ. Transferring that result to a particular cell lineage in DHQ1 would require corresponding cell- or layer-resolved evidence. Goel et al. provide a direct tree example of tissue-dependent capture in apricot. In their comparison of fruit-layer candidates with adjacent leaves, none of 123 L1-exclusive candidates and 49 of 70 L2-exclusive candidates were called in the leaves. Those leaf fractions apply to candidates already found in layer-enriched fruit samples. [Arabidopsis lineage study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-026-02264-1) · [Apricot tissue-layer study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03337-0)

# Which historical studies measure a successful heritable outcome?

A paper-index search on 19 November 2026 covered 1 January 2025 through the search date. One query sought perennial-plant mosaic mutations, orthogonal validation and progeny. A second sought time-resolved somatic mutation, cell lineage and reproductive outcomes. Each returned 14 ranked titles. These searches led to the Tempranillo workbook and to an independent conifer family study. The search was targeted and earlier work might also qualify. [Grapevine paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/hr/uhaf237) · [Loblolly pine study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41437-025-00814-5)

Guner and colleagues measured a transmitted phenotype in loblolly pine. Among the seven field branch samples whose seeds were collected, two (WB-04 and WB-06) produced 0.0% dwarf seedlings in the reported phenotype table. The other five produced 24.2% to 53.8% dwarf seedlings. The table also reports three cultivated Nana mothers with 50.0% to 55.8%. In families showing segregation, linkage mapping identified a broad region on chromosome linkage group eight. The authors explicitly leave open whether the causal gene or genes are the same across families. Branches were selected because of their visible morphology. The observed segregation addresses transmission. Neither identified alternative causal alleles nor times of first qualifying events in a prespecified at-risk cohort are supplied. [Table 1, Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12811388/)

For the focal peach leaf DHQ1_B1-1-L1, the Wang study reports Sanger confirmation of 118 of 122 previously called positive measurements and no mutant alleles in 274 controls across its study. The study-wide Methods do not identify the M101–leaf combination among those control measurements. The peach progeny in S2 Table come from tree GZ, and S5 Table and S6 Fig. concern tree HY1. The targeted Ji counts on later DHQ1 flower material refer to different biological specimens. None of these passages supplies an independent absence call for M101 in the sampled 2016 leaf. [Wang et al., Methods and Supporting Information](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191) · [Ji et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011829)

The pine study received more than 40 public reports of unusual branches. The authors selected seven that had cones and could be reached. They also collected cones from adjacent normal branches and reported that those progeny showed no segregation for dwarfism. These design details identify both the observed inheritance contrast and the selection step before measurement.

There are two distinct denominators even within one pine family. For WB-10, the Results report 78 dwarf and 67 normal offspring, giving 78/145 or 53.8%. Table 1 reports the same percentage and a sample size of 120 next to the genotyping platform and marker count. The phenotypic fraction can be recovered directly from the 145 observed outcomes. Substituting 120 for the phenotyping denominator would give 65%. Five of seven sampled field families produced dwarf progeny in this grow-out. The field branches were chosen from more than 40 reports for availability of cones and ease of access. These sampling units support a heritable phenotypic outcome in particular families, while the identity and first arrival time of the causal change remain open.

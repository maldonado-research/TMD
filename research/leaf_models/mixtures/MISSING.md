# Which apparent separations survive a missed bulk call?

The exact bulk model makes a strong claim for the peach leaf DHQ1_B1-1-L2. Its three recorded markers M71, M92 and M101 must occupy separate cellular paths if each marker arose once, persisted in descendants, and every bulk call recorded whether any cells carried it. We asked how easily one missed bulk membership could change that conclusion. The costs here count added bulk entries and allow any number of cellular patterns within a leaf. [Original samples and markers](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000191.s019) · [Exact-call model](ROW_SPLIT.md)

Let A and B denote the observed sets of leaves for two markers. If the A mutation occurred on the ancestral path to B, every B-bearing cell also carries A. In a bulk union with complete detection, every B-positive leaf must then list A. Retaining all listed calls requires at least |B ∖ A| additions to A. That cost also suffices. Add A to exactly those leaves, put B below A on one cellular branch, and put the other markers on separate branches. At each leaf, choose the paths ending at its listed markers. Their union is exactly its completed row. The opposite direction costs |A ∖ B|. This construction tests the possibility of a shared cellular path. It imposes no limit on how many paths a leaf may contain.

Among the ten originally crossing marker pairs, **seven** can share a cellular path after adding just one bulk membership, **two** require two, and **one** requires three. Those are separate pairwise questions. A different addition may be required for each pair. [Full directional costs](one_missing_result.json)

For M101 to precede M71, the only required addition is M101 at **DHQ1_B1-1-L1**. For M71 to precede M101, the 13 M101-only leaves would each need an M71 addition. To see what the single hypothetical change does, make it and leave the other 3,599 entries intact. There is now a fit to all 75 leaves using **99** signature occurrences overall, with M71 and M101 on a shared cellular path in L2. They can also remain separate on a single common tree, which needs at least **101** occurrences. Both bounds are attained. The two extra signatures in the latter case occur in L1 and L2. A positive bulk measurement at L1 would therefore allow both cellular arrangements. It could not by itself decide whether M71 and M101 occur in the same cell in L2. [Witnesses and lower bounds](one_missing_call.py)

Starting from the unedited table, an independently confirmed double-positive cell in L2 would contradict the combination of complete bulk calls, one origin per marker and permanent inheritance. Under the additional restriction of at most one missed bulk membership and no false positives, the same observation would require the M101 addition at L1. The analysis establishes no such within-cell observation or error budget. Ji et al. had already assigned M101 and M71 to different groups using tissues from the same tree years later.
